PeckList is a task app for you and the people you share a home, a trip or a project with. Your lists live on a server you run yourself, every device keeps a full copy, and the app works offline: changes sync within seconds once it is back online.

<b>You need a PeckList server.</b> The app connects to one: run your own with Docker, Linux packages or Windows (it is free software, AGPL-3.0), or use one someone runs for you. You can also use the app on this device only, with no account and no sync. To try it first, sign up on the demo server at demo.pecklist.com, which starts over on a schedule.

<b>Plan your day</b>
<ul>
<li>Today, Upcoming and a calendar by year, month or a few days</li>
<li>Quick add that reads dates, times, repeats, tags and people from what you type</li>
<li>Lists, subtasks, tags, priorities, start and due dates</li>
<li>Repeating tasks that move on to their next date when you complete them</li>
<li>A compact row size for a long Today, and 30 color palettes in light or dark</li>
</ul>

<b>Reminders that arrive</b>
<ul>
<li>Reminders come on time with the app closed</li>
<li>Complete and Snooze right on the notification</li>
<li>Constant reminders for the tasks that matter: they keep buzzing until you act</li>
</ul>

<b>Share the load</b>
<ul>
<li>Share a list with friends and groups, who can view it or edit it</li>
<li>Assign a task to someone, or let a group's members call dibs</li>
<li>Comments with @mentions, and files on any task</li>
<li>A notification when someone shares a list, assigns you a task or mentions you</li>
</ul>

<b>On your phone</b>
<ul>
<li>Widgets: Today, Today's count, and Add task buttons</li>
<li>Add a task by voice, from a Quick Settings tile, or by sharing text from another app</li>
<li>Long-press the icon for Add task, Today and Search</li>
<li>Follows your phone's light or dark mode, with a themed icon on Android 13 and later</li>
</ul>

<b>Bring your tasks along</b>
<ul>
<li>Import from TickTick and Todoist</li>
<li>iCalendar import and export</li>
<li>A task API with API keys, for scripts, Home Assistant and Obsidian</li>
</ul>

<b>Sign in your way</b>
Sign-in happens in your own browser, so passkeys and single sign-on through your own identity provider work.

<b>Privacy</b>
The app talks only to the server you connect it to. If it crashes, it offers to send a crash report, which you can read first; it goes through your server to the project, with no task names or personal details in it. You can turn reports off for all your devices, and a server admin can turn them off for everyone.
