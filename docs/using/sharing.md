# Sharing

Sharing needs a PeckList server: everyone on it has an account. Friends, groups and your friend
link are on the **Friends** page, in the account menu (the sync button).

## Friends

You share with friends, so start there:

- **Your friend link**: copy it, or show its QR code for someone to scan with their phone. They
  sign in, accept, and you are friends. **New link** stops the old one from working.
- **Add a friend**: type their exact username and **Ask to be friends**. They accept or decline under
  **Requests**; you can take a request back while it waits.
- **Unfriend** ends it. If they are on lists you own, or have tasks of yours assigned to them, it
  also offers to remove them from those, ticked by default. Leave it ticked and those disappear from
  their devices; untick it and the shares stay. Lists they own or that you only joined stay as they
  are, and so does anything they reach through a group. Anyone with your friend link, including
  someone you unfriended, can use it again to become friends, unless you press **New link**.

An admin can also make two people friends, or end a friendship (**Admin > Users > Friends**). An
admin ending it ends only the friendship: lists and tasks they share stay shared, and the owner
removes the person from those if they should go.

The server holds to this too: a list, a task or a group invite goes only to your friends, to people
in a group you are in, and to people on a shared list with you. Anyone else is asked to be friends
first (assigning a task to someone you found by name does that for you).

## Groups

A group shares lists and chores with several people at once. **New group**, then **Invite a
friend**; they **Join** from **Group invites**. The owner can **Make owner** someone else. When the
owner leaves, the next member becomes the owner; when the last member leaves, the group is archived.
While someone invited has not answered yet, it waits for them instead: the first to accept becomes
the owner.

**Server groups** are made by an admin and appear here marked as such: you can share with them and
assign to them, but only admins change who is in them.

## Sharing a list

Open the list's **Share** button (on a phone it is in the list's **...** menu):

| Role         | Can                                                    |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Owner**    | Everything, including who is in and deleting the list. |
| **Can edit** | Add, change, complete and delete tasks.                |
| **Can view** | See the list and its tasks.                            |

Add friends or groups, change a role, or remove someone. **Make private** takes the list back from
everyone (it disappears from their devices). Someone it is shared with can **Leave list**.

A person you remove (or everyone, after **Make private**) still keeps a task of the list that is
assigned to them, so they can finish it: they see and can change that task, its subtasks and its
comments, but cannot move it to another list, and see nothing else on the list. To take it back
too, unassign them. [What you share](#what-you-share) lists these tasks.

Shared lists appear under **Shared with me** for the others. Changes reach everyone within seconds,
and offline edits merge when the device reconnects.

## Assigning

In a shared list, give a task to someone (or a group) from its details, or type `@name` in
[quick add](quick-add-and-shortcuts.md). **Assigned to me** collects everything given to you.
A task given to a group is anyone's in it: **Dibs** takes it, and so does assigning one of its
members. Either way the group comes off, so everyone sees who has it.

## What you share

At the bottom of **Friends and groups** (profile menu), **What you share** lists everything you
share, by person and then by group: each shared list with the role it gives, and each task you
assigned to someone who is not on its list. Tap one to open it. The cross stops sharing it with that
person or group (removes them from the list, or unassigns them), and **Undo** brings it back.
